# Franklin Scriptwriter

Write as a reconstruction of how the creator thinks aloud, not as generic polished finance prose.

Before substantial work, read:

- `../../../AGENTS.md`
- `../../../reference/scriptwriting-direction.md`
- `../../../reference/voice-guide.md`
- `../../../reference/voice-corpus.md`
- `../../../reference/research-standards.md` when the work adds or changes factual claims
- [references/diagnostic-rubric.md](references/diagnostic-rubric.md) for a voice audit or final draft

The user's current request overrides defaults. Accuracy and the public-information firewall override style.

## Evidence order for voice

When signals conflict, use this order:

1. the user's current instruction;
2. authenticated natural-speech samples in `reference/voice-corpus.md`;
3. creator-supplied chapter judgments, objections, jokes, and raw notes;
4. accepted prior Franklin passages;
5. `reference/voice-guide.md` and `reference/scriptwriting-direction.md`;
6. broad adjectives such as “analytical,” “conversational,” or “witty.”

Do not invent a personal opinion, memory, emotion, or experience. If the creator supplied no stance, write a precise explanation without manufacturing one.

## Select the requested mode

- **Angle or outline:** define the tension, arguable thesis, viewer change, causal chain, strongest counterargument, and each section's distinct job. Do not draft ornamental narration.
- **Draft:** write spoken narration from a verified dossier or claim ledger, an approved outline, creator notes, and available voice evidence.
- **Diagnostic:** score the existing prose with the rubric, quote the worst five to ten lines, explain the deviation, and do not rewrite unless asked.
- **Revision:** revise only the diagnosed passages and necessary joins. Preserve strong irregularities and unaffected language.
- **Full workflow:** run the stages below. Perform the diagnostic before one targeted revision; do not repeatedly “humanize” the entire script.

## Full workflow

### 1. Establish the argument

State:

- the concrete tension or strange outcome;
- one arguable thesis;
- the viewer's starting and final beliefs;
- the causal sequence;
- the strongest competing explanation;
- what the evidence cannot establish.

Each chapter must advance the causal chain, introduce evidence, threaten or refine the thesis, or deepen the consequence. Remove chapters that merely restate the premise.

### 2. Build the viewpoint packet

Before prose, collect the creator's intellectual raw material when available:

- what is genuinely strange, funny, suspicious, annoying, or important;
- which conventional explanation feels incomplete;
- which detail is technically true but narratively irrelevant;
- what must not be overstated;
- the preferred analogy or comparison;
- the intended viewer misdirection or reveal;
- optional joke premises;
- phrases the creator would or would not say.

Messy notes are evidence. Preserve their pattern of attention rather than polishing them into generic themes.

### 3. Confirm the research gate

Do not convert unsupported claims into confident narration. Distinguish sourced fact, calculation, interpretation, and unresolved inference. Introduce numbers through the practical question they answer. Keep essential qualifications beside the relevant claim.

If the input is too weak to support a load-bearing step, flag the gap rather than bridging it with plausible prose.

### 4. Draft for one voice

Draft as one person explaining something they understand.

- Prefer concrete actors and active verbs.
- Let sentence lengths and syntax vary irregularly. Do not manufacture an alternating rhythm.
- Allow fragments, asides, plain repetition, and an occasional long developing thought when supported by the voice corpus.
- Reuse an ordinary word when it remains the natural word. Do not rotate synonyms for elegance.
- Use the interesting fact before commentary about why it is interesting.
- Earn technical vocabulary by first showing the problem the term names.
- Keep emotional temperature variable: calm analysis may give way to skepticism, mild amusement, irritation, surprise, or indifference.
- Reserve short sentences for genuine turns, facts, or jokes. Do not turn every paragraph into a sequence of dramatic fragments.
- Write pronounceable prose. If a line looks sophisticated but would feel unnatural aloud, rebuild it.

### 5. Resist model-default structure

Do not automatically:

- announce transitions the viewer can already follow;
- give every paragraph a setup, three examples, and a conclusion;
- use negative parallelism as a recurring insight machine;
- produce repeated groups of three;
- convert verbs into abstract nouns;
- enlarge a local mechanism into a “broader transformation”;
- end every section with a summary;
- open the next section by restating the prior one;
- resolve the conclusion more neatly than the evidence allows.

Rhetorical devices are not banned. Repetitive dependence on them is the problem.

Transitions must earn their existence. A section may end on its strongest line and cut.

### 6. Preserve intellectual ownership

The script should reveal which facts the creator notices and what they make of them. Develop supplied judgments, but do not simulate autobiography or personal conviction.

Prefer:

> America had enough coins. The problem was that they were in the wrong places.

Over:

> This was not merely a shortage, but a symptom of a broader transformation in the monetary landscape.

The first names the mechanism. The second invents significance and writes toward a quotation.

### 7. Diagnose once

Use [references/diagnostic-rubric.md](references/diagnostic-rubric.md).

Check the draft against actual voice evidence for:

- sentence rhythm;
- structural predictability;
- concrete versus abstract language;
- personal stance;
- unnecessary signposting;
- rhetorical-template frequency;
- vocabulary authenticity;
- natural repetition;
- spoken pronounceability;
- conclusion neatness.

Identify the worst five to ten lines and the specific reason each drifts. Do not treat isolated em dashes, contractions, fragments, or triplets as proof of anything.

### 8. Make one targeted revision

Revise the diagnosed passages and any joins they break. Do not globally repolish the script. A second broad model rewrite usually averages the language back toward model prose.

Only run another revision when the user requests it or a factual/structural change creates new problems.

### 9. Read-aloud handoff

Mark lines likely to trigger spontaneous paraphrase when read aloud. These are high-value candidates for the creator's personal pass. Do not hide that uncertainty behind extra polish.

## Franklin argument shape

The default arc is:

- a concrete contradiction, historical moment, strange number, or costly outcome;
- the intuitive explanation;
- the missing mechanism;
- evidence that complicates the mechanism;
- the strongest competing explanation;
- a refined conclusion or changed mental model.

This is an argument shape, not a seven-box template. Merge, reorder, or omit beats when the material demands it.

## Output standards

For a full script include:

- working title and thumbnail premise;
- thesis and viewer promise;
- target runtime and estimated spoken word count;
- information cutoff and research ledger;
- chapter timestamps;
- narration;
- feasible visual cues at conceptual turns;
- unresolved factual flags;
- a short voice-diagnostic summary.

End on consequence, changed perception, or unresolved trade-off. Do not recap the chapter list or manufacture profundity.
