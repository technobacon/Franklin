# Franklin — Scriptwriting Direction

## The doctrine

Franklin scripts should not aim for generic excellence. They should reconstruct how the creator naturally explains an interesting idea when he knows what he is talking about.

“Human” is not a synonym for casual, chatty, witty, slang-heavy, or imperfect. It comes from a recognizable distribution of habits: where the narrator pays attention, how a thought develops, which words recur, when skepticism appears, which qualifications matter, and where the sentence stops.

The operating principle is:

> Use specific voice evidence, preserve its irregularities, and remove structural habits the model introduces.

Research establishes facts and arguments. Voice comes from the creator's pattern of attention.

## Franklin's editorial identity

Franklin explains economics, financial systems, personal finance, and financial history through a calm modern analyst examining evidence that is often older, stranger, or less tidy than the conventional account.

The viewer should feel spoken with, not lectured at. The narration is:

- knowledgeable without credential theatre;
- calm but not emotionally flat;
- skeptical without reflexive contrarianism;
- precise without becoming noun-heavy;
- dryly amused when incentives produce absurd outcomes;
- willing to leave a complication unresolved;
- interested in mechanisms more than labels.

Every video needs one arguable thesis. The hook should create a real question in the first 15–30 seconds. Evidence should escalate, and the strongest counterargument must be able to damage or refine the thesis. The ending should produce a changed mental model, consequence, or unresolved trade-off—not a recap.

## What the script is modelling

The target is not polished written prose. It is how the creator constructs thoughts while speaking coherently.

A useful script may contain:

- a very short sentence after a dense explanation;
- another short sentence, if the thought really lands that way;
- a long sentence with an aside;
- a fragment;
- an ordinary word repeated several times;
- a qualification that interrupts a clean rhetorical line;
- a transition omitted because the logic is already visible.

Do not impose irregularity as a new pattern. A predictable short-long-short rhythm is still predictable.

## Voice evidence hierarchy

Use, in order:

1. current instructions from the creator;
2. transcripts of the creator speaking naturally;
3. spontaneous substitutions made during a read-aloud;
4. creator-supplied chapter notes and judgments;
5. accepted Franklin script passages;
6. the formal voice guide;
7. broad labels such as analytical, conversational, dry, or human.

Examples outrank adjectives. Do not “interpret” the voice when evidence can be inferred from real samples.

A passage belongs in the voice corpus because the creator would actually say it, not merely because it is well written.

## The three roles

### Researcher

The researcher establishes the factual record and causal possibilities without optimizing for voice.

The output separates:

- sourced fact;
- calculation;
- interpretation;
- disputed explanation;
- unresolved evidence;
- tempting overclaim.

The researcher does not add importance language to make a fact feel cinematic.

### Writer

The writer receives:

- the verified dossier and claim ledger;
- the approved thesis and argument map;
- the creator's raw intellectual notes;
- relevant voice samples;
- the script constraints and target runtime.

The writer develops the supplied judgments. It does not invent a personal anecdote, emotion, or conviction.

### Editor

The editor first diagnoses deviations from the voice corpus. It does not rewrite while diagnosing.

The editor scores:

- sentence rhythm;
- structural predictability;
- concrete versus abstract language;
- personal stance;
- unnecessary signposting;
- rhetorical-template frequency;
- vocabulary authenticity;
- natural repetition;
- spoken pronounceability;
- conclusion neatness.

It quotes the worst five to ten lines, explains why they drift, and defines the smallest revision boundary. Then one targeted revision is made.

Do not run serial “more natural,” “more conversational,” “less AI,” and “flow better” passes. Repeated broad rewrites tend to average the prose back toward model defaults.

## The creator's raw-notes packet

Before drafting a full script, preserve unpolished comments such as:

- “This is the part I find strange.”
- “The usual explanation skips this step.”
- “Technically true, but irrelevant to the actual mechanism.”
- “Do not overstate this.”
- “I want the viewer to assume X until the reveal.”
- “The comparison here is inventories, not money supply.”
- “Maybe a joke about everyone keeping billions in sofa cushions.”
- “I am not convinced by the conventional account.”
- “This should feel cold, not dramatic.”

These notes show which facts the creator notices. They are more valuable than generic requests to sound witty or natural.

If no such notes exist, the script may still be clear and precise. It must not manufacture intellectual ownership.

## Language direction

Prefer agents doing things over abstract concepts undergoing processes.

Prefer:

> Banks were not getting coins back.

Avoid:

> The return flow of coinage into banking institutions declined.

Prefer:

> People had not stopped using money. They had stopped moving coins around in the way the system expected them to.

Avoid:

> This phenomenon reflected a broader shift in consumer behaviour and monetary circulation.

Use subjects and verbs. A necessary formal noun is not forbidden; a chain of them is a warning.

Do not replace a repeated ordinary word with elegant variation merely to demonstrate vocabulary. If “problem” remains the accurate word, keep using “problem.”

Introduce technical terms after the practical problem and mechanism are clear. Introduce a number through the question it answers, then state the unit, period, denominator, and qualification.

## Structural direction

Each section needs a distinct job, but not the same internal shape.

Do not automatically:

- announce what the viewer is about to learn;
- explain that the script is moving to another part;
- give every idea three examples;
- end every section by restating its lesson;
- begin the next section by repeating the previous ending;
- make every objection arrive at the same timestamp;
- wrap every argument in a polished conclusion.

A transition is useful when chronology jumps, the actor changes, or the causal link would otherwise be ambiguous. If sentence B naturally follows sentence A, move to B.

A section can end here:

> The coins had not vanished. They had stopped circulating.

Cut.

## Rhetorical direction

No single construction is banned. Reliance is the problem.

Watch for repeated:

- “It is not X. It is Y.”
- “It is not just X, but Y.”
- “Here is the thing.”
- “But here is where it gets interesting.”
- “So what does this mean?”
- one-word questions answered immediately;
- groups of three;
- quote-shaped aphorisms;
- em dashes used as automatic drama;
- conclusions that announce a “broader lesson.”

Do not repair a stock construction by swapping vocabulary. Rebuild the thought around the mechanism.

## Originality and stance

Safe interpretation is often more damaging than formal vocabulary.

Generic:

> This demonstrates how disruptions in circulation can create shortages even when aggregate supply remains sufficient.

Franklin:

> America did not run out of coins. In a bizarre way, it ran out of moving coins.

The second sentence contains an interpretation. It notices the strange part and compresses the mechanism. That kind of line should come from creator-supplied judgment or a clearly approved channel thesis, not an invented claim to personal feeling.

Emotional range for Franklin can include skepticism, mild amusement, annoyance, surprise, suspicion, or deliberate indifference. It should remain proportionate to the event. Harm, insolvency, unemployment, or personal loss is not a stage for a gag.

## Negative examples as a decision boundary

Maintain paired examples in the voice corpus.

Rejected:

> This was not merely a shortage of coins—it was a symptom of a deeper transformation in how Americans interacted with money.

Accepted:

> America had enough coins. The problem was that they were in the wrong places.

Reason: the rejected line invents significance, relies on negative parallelism, turns a local mechanism into an abstract transformation, and tries to sound quotable. The accepted line names the mechanism in ordinary words.

The explanation of the difference matters as much as the pair.

## The spoken test

The draft is for one person saying the words aloud.

A sentence fails when it is:

- difficult to pronounce cleanly;
- overloaded with subordinate clauses;
- front-loaded with abstractions;
- qualified long after the claim;
- built around a number the listener cannot contextualize;
- sophisticated on the page but implausible in the creator's mouth.

During the final read, highlight every sentence the creator instinctively paraphrases. Preserve the spontaneous version. It is fresh voice evidence.

Do not simulate humanity with deliberate typos, random slang, weak grammar, excessive contractions, or arbitrary profanity.

## Revision protocol

1. Complete the factual and structural draft.
2. Diagnose specific voice drift using the rubric.
3. Define the smallest revision boundary.
4. Make one targeted voice revision.
5. Read aloud.
6. Capture spontaneous creator substitutions for the corpus.
7. Make human edits or a narrowly requested follow-up.

A factual repair may happen at any time. It should trigger a voice check only for the changed passage and its joins, not a global rewrite.

## Definition of done

A Franklin script is ready for the creator's voice pass when:

- the thesis is arguable and the evidence can support it;
- every chapter contributes a new causal step, objection, or consequence;
- facts, calculations, and interpretations remain distinguishable;
- the strongest competing explanation is treated fairly;
- actual voice evidence outranks style adjectives;
- sentence rhythm is irregular without becoming performative;
- concrete actors and verbs dominate abstract framing;
- repetition serves speech rather than padding;
- transitions and summaries have earned their space;
- humour reveals character or mechanism;
- no personal stance has been fabricated;
- the ending lands on a consequence or changed model;
- the diagnostic has been run once and the revision was targeted;
- the script remains easy for a solo creator to narrate and edit.
