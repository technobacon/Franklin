## Visual thesis

The film should feel like an old financial archive being calmly opened by a modern analyst. The archive is not decoration: names, dates, seals, addresses, and missing certificates are the plot. Stock footage supplies physical reality and breathing room. Generated material supplies atmosphere and modular props, never counterfeit evidence.

## Non-negotiables

1. Do not generate faces for any named child.
2. Do not imply that Anne Audéoud is on the January 1782 list.
3. Do not imply that the twenty-nine girls counted in October 1784 are the same cohort as the January 1782 list.
4. Never present an AI-generated letter, certificate, ledger, signature, or seal as a real historical document.
5. Add all legible names, dates, sums, citations, and labels in Resolve/Fusion; image generators should produce no text.
6. The Pernette section receives no horror sting, heartbeat, mascot joke, or melodramatic child reconstruction.
7. Use France only when the payment mechanism needs explaining. The visual story remains in Geneva and with the girls.
8. Every archival asset must retain a source record and a saved licence/rights page.

## Franklin treatment

- Canvas: warm ivory paper `#E7E0D2` or warm white `#F4F0E8`.
- Ink: `#191919`; secondary charcoal `#2B2B2B`; quiet grey `#8C8981`.
- Accents: banknote green `#526B5A`; muted gold `#A88D57`; muted red only for a stopped payment or confirmed death.
- Type: EB Garamond for names, dates, chapter cards, and quotations; Inter or IBM Plex Sans for source labels and modern diagrams.
- Texture: 3–8% paper grain. Avoid heavy grunge.
- Motion: slow 4–8 second pushes, controlled parallax, deliberate underlines, short map routes. One dominant object per frame.
- Mascot: four small appearances at most; never over primary evidence and never during a child's death.
